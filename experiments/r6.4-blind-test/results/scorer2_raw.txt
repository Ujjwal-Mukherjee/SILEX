### Final assessment of the scoring

Three principles guided these judgments.

First, technical validity is not semantic correctness. In Test 3, every answer passed the checker, yet several expressions still lost meaning.

Second, I treated correct conclusions with incomplete reasoning according to the keys. In particular, the no-reference readers were not penalized merely for lacking the reference. They were penalized only where their answers missed required distinctions.

Third, the uncertain judgments are identified rather than silently resolved. These involve genuinely borderline interpretations, not assumptions about the identities or capabilities of the participants.

One adjustment to the provisional Test 1 judgment is warranted: W1 R14 can reasonably receive 2 rather than 1, because “if the training had finished” already conveys a counterfactual reading. On a meaning-focused application of the key, I would award 2.

The strongest evidence of limitations in the submitted answers concerns counterfactuals, concurrent messages, attribution of causal outcomes, and universal negation. These are more informative than superficial wording differences.

I have kept the three tests separate, with no averaging or ranking of W1–W4, as requested by the scoring instructions.
